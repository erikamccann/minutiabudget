Frame as **"Builds the system, not just works inside it — with the financial literacy to make the business case."**

Most IT/digital managers in energy either come from the business (and struggle with IT governance) or come from IT (and can't speak to operations). Erika does both — and goes further: she can build the governance framework, present it to a SteerCo, chair the resulting committees, and track platform adoption metrics, all while connecting the work back to capital spend and OPEX reduction. That full-stack delivery leadership is rare at Manager level.

For Product Manager roles: the differentiator is business acumen and financial literacy that most PMs don't have. She built the business case showing $432M in capital and 37% of OPEX depend on the platform her team governs. She tracks adoption (506 users, 82 avg daily logins) and frames it as enterprise risk. That's product thinking, not just delivery execution.

**Process-first design** is a third differentiator that cuts across all archetypes. Most EAM and IT programs fail because they configure the system first and discover the process gaps later — in production. Erika advocates for and enforces the opposite: define the business outcome, map the workflow, assign organizational accountability, establish governance — then configure the system to match. This is the discipline behind the 9 design tenets, the Azure DevOps work hierarchy (Epics → Features → User Stories → Test Cases → Defects), and the work management designs (work order and inspection workflows built around how work actually gets created, approved, executed, and closed — not how the software wants it to happen).

**The translator advantage:** sits at the intersection of Operations, Engineering, IT, and Digital. Can speak maintenance process with a field supervisor, present a hosting-model decision to a CFO, and write a work item hierarchy for a DevOps team. That translation fluency — in all four directions — is what makes the governance work stick.
